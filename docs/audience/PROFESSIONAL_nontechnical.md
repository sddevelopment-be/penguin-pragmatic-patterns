# Persona: Context-Seeking Partner

## Overview

> **Purpose:** Represent non-technical professionals (operations, marketing, finance) who collaborate with engineering teams.
> They browse the repository to grasp terminology and decision logic without diving into code.

* **Role Focus:** Translate business objectives into cross-functional plans.
* **Primary Function:** Coordinate launches, budgets, or campaigns that depend on engineering outputs.
* **Environment / Context:** Matrixed organizations where technical and business tracks often misalign.

---

## Core Motivations

* **Professional Drivers:** Make commitments grounded in real capability; reduce miscommunication.
* **Emotional or Cognitive Drivers:** Curious generalist; values clarity and predictability.
* **Systemic Positioning:** Acts as liaison—represents business needs to tech teams and vice versa.

---

## Desiderata

| Category    | Expectation / Need        | Description                                                       |
|-------------|---------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Information | Plain-language primers    | Needs accessible explanations of concepts, constraints, and risks.|
| Interaction | Structured checkpoints    | Prefers recurring syncs with shared agendas and visual artifacts.  |
| Support     | Decision framing tools    | Looks for canvases/checklists to prep proposals or trade-offs.     |
| Governance  | Clarity on ownership      | Wants to know who approves what, avoiding escalation ping-pong.    |

---

## Frustrations and Constraints

* **Pain Points:** Tech jargon blocking understanding; shifting priorities causing rework.
* **Trade-Off Awareness:** Balances market deadlines with engineering realities; negotiates scope often.
* **Environmental Constraints:** Limited access to detailed roadmaps; depends on proxies for velocity/quality.

---

## Behavioral Cues

| Situation            | Typical Behavior                                 | Interpretation                                      |
|----------------------|--------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Stable / Routine     | Builds narratives, updates exec decks            | Turns technical progress into stakeholder stories.   |
| Change / Uncertainty | Asks for clarity, seeks analogies                 | Uses knowledge base to translate new concepts.       |
| Under Pressure       | Escalates for answers, risks oversimplifying     | Needs timely context to avoid misalignment.          |

---

## Collaboration Preferences

* **Decision Style:** Outcome-driven; ensures constraints are explicit before finalizing plans.
* **Communication Style:** Executive summaries backed by detail-on-demand; appreciates diagrams and timelines.
* **Feedback Expectations:** Values empathetic, jargon-free explanations; provides reciprocal insights from customer/business side.

---

## Measures of Success

| Dimension   | Indicator                                      | Type                                   |
|-------------|------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Performance | On-time launches without emergency escalations | Quantitative (delivery/marketing KPIs) |
| Quality     | Reduced misunderstanding between teams         | Qualitative (stakeholder feedback)     |
| Growth      | Increased fluency in technical discussions     | Developmental (self/peer assessment)   |

---

## Cross-Context Adaptation

| Domain    | Specific Focus             | Adaptation Notes                                        |
|-----------|----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| Technical | Dependency awareness       | Uses primers to ask better questions in planning.       |
| Service   | Customer-facing narratives | Adapts concepts into messaging or finance briefings.    |

---

## Narrative Summary

A cross-functional partner who wants to collaborate without slowing teams down. They rely on Pragmatic Penguin to decode terms like “practice,” “concept,” or “primer,” and to build trust by showing they respect the craft—even if they don’t write code.
