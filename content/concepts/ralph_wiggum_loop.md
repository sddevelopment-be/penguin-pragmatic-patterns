## Definition

The Ralph Wiggum Loop is a structured self-observation cycle applied during execution to detect drift, confusion, or misalignment and trigger timely course correction.

## Key Components

- Observation checkpoint: explicit pause during ongoing work.
- Warning-sign scan: detect scope drift, verbosity, uncertainty, or directive mismatch.
- Reflection decision: continue, adjust, or escalate.
- Correction action: narrow scope, reload context, or request clarification.

## Background

### Origin

The concept emerged in agentic workflow guidance as a practical response to long-running task drift and mode misuse.

### Application

It is used in multi-step tasks, high-context transitions, and long sessions where silent drift is likely. Teams embed checkpoints at fixed intervals or trigger-based moments.

### Comparisons

Unlike end-of-task retrospectives, this loop is in-flight and preventive. Unlike static checklists, it requires active diagnosis and decision-making.

## Examples

During a long content migration, an agent notices repeated speculative additions and pauses. It re-reads the task objective, removes non-requested work, and resumes with a narrowed scope.
